applications for a joint tenure-track faculty position at the rank of Assistant Professor, with an anticipated start date of August 31, 2027. We seek a transnational American Studies scholar of comparative and multi-sited Global Souths (especially Latin America, and/or Southeast Asia, and/or the U.S. South) whose interdisciplinary research draws on settler colonial studies and relational race/ethnic studies in order

to consider the legacies of the U.S.’s political role in the global flows and functions of race, empire, war, colonialism, and capital in the twentieth century and twenty-first centuries . We are particularly interested in a scholar with expertise in environmental humanities, critical refugee studies (with an emphasis on Southeast Asia), and/or transoceanic studies who looks at how agribusiness (principally, farming

and fisheries), technology, and/or cultural objects and materials inform (and are informed by) those and other processes (like diaspora, nation-state formation, and refugee lifeworlds/deathworlds). Thus, we seek a scholar whose work explores histories of wartime counterinsurgency networks in the Global South and the ways that they shape contemporary (broadly construed) landscapes and discourses of structures like the

nation, policing, surveillance, border enforcement, displacement, dispossession, and forced migration in the United States. Preferred candidates will be scholars whose research substantively engages with studies of race, gender, class, sexuality, and/or disability; who have a background in interdisciplinary studies (i.e., American Studies); who have evidence of interest in digital humanities; and whose work holds
