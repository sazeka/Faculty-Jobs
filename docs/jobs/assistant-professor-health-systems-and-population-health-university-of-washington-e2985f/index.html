Assistant Professor, Health Systems and Population Health Position Overview School / Campus / College: School of Public Health Organization: Health Systems and Population Health Title: Assistant Professor, Health Systems and Population Health Position Details Position Description The Department of Health Systems and Population Health (HSPop) at the University of Washington invites applications for a faculty position

at the rank of Assistant Professor, tenure track, with an earliest anticipated start date of April 1, 2027. We seek applicants who demonstrate commitment to excellence in research, teaching, and service in a diverse and inclusive academic community. The successful candidate will join our faculty on a full-time basis for a 12-month appointment, 3-year term, renewable for a second 3-year term. The base salary range for

this position will be $10,000 - $12,500 per month, commensurate with experience and qualifications, or as mandated by a U.S. Department of Labor prevailing wage determination. The successful candidate will be expected to: Collaboratively advance the scholarly portfolio of the Anti-Racism and Community Health (ARCH) Center through interdisciplinary and community-engaged research and evaluation focused on disrupting

structural racism and discrimination, strengthening institutional capacity for engaging community partners, and advancing community-led solutions for health equity. Contribute to educational leadership by developing and delivering high-quality undergraduate and graduate instruction, including the expected course load, and contributing to course and curriculum development. Use or develop innovative and evidence-based

pedagogy that integrates equity, diversity, inclusion, belonging, anti-racism, and/or decolonizing scholarly frameworks; Maintain an active, high-impact research and scholarship agenda, including pursuing extramural funding and conducting and disseminating research and evaluation. Scholarship should integrate methodologies to study anti-racism and health equity. Examples include work focused on public safety and

alternatives to policing, carceral system impacts, immigrant and refugee health, Black health and well-being, racial trauma and healing, LGBTQ+ health, Indigenous and social determinants of health. Mentor, advise, and supervise undergraduate and graduate students and foster an inclusive learning environment that supports student success.
