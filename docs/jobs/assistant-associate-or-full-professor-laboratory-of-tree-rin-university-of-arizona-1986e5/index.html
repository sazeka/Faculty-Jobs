Tucson Campus Address Tucson, AZ USA Position Highlights The Laboratory of Tree-Ring Research (LTRR) and the School of Anthropology (SoA) at the University of Arizona invite applications for the Emil Haury Endowed Chair in Archaeological Dendrochronology. This is an open-rank search for a tenure-track/tenured faculty position, starting in August 2027. We seek a dendrochronologist to advance and support the field of

archaeological dendrochronology via a split appointment in LTRR and SoA. We are looking for a scholar strongly committed to interdisciplinary engagement who will strengthen existing research emphases and/or advance new topics of research, building on key strengths in both departments. A strong commitment to teaching and mentoring students, evidenced by career trajectory and/or track record, is essential. Previous

field and/or specialist laboratory experience would be an advantage. Applicants working with ancestral communities in the Americas are particularly encouraged, yet we also welcome strong applications from scholars engaged in any area of archaeological dendrochronology. Applications should be submitted through talent by December 31, 2026. The Laboratory of Tree-Ring Research is a hub of interdisciplinary research,

teaching, and outreach at the University of Arizona. Established in 1937, LTRR formalized the scientific study of tree rings in America, pioneering the application of dendroarchaeology in the American Southwest. Using the information preserved in the annual rings of trees, LTRR researchers work to better understand and quantify the complex interactions among Earth's climate, ecosystems, and societies. Since 2024, the

LTRR has expanded its analytical capacity in wood anatomy, stable isotope, and radiocarbon analysis. The laboratory is dedicated to developing and advancing novel approaches to address scientific questions of societal relevance while sharing these discoveries and their impacts with researchers, students, the public, resource managers, and policymakers.
