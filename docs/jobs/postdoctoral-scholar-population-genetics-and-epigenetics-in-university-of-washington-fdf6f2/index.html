Postdoctoral Scholar: Population genetics and epigenetics in the Atlantic Sea Scallop Position Overview School / Campus / College: College of the Environment Organization: Aquatic and Fishery Sciences Title: Postdoctoral Scholar: Population genetics and epigenetics in the Atlantic Sea Scallop Position Details Position Description The School of Aquatic and Fishery Science in collaboration with Dr Samuel Gurr at

Hatfield Marine Science Center (HMSC, Oregon State University) invites applications for a full-time Postdoctoral Scholar (PDS) to lead bioinformatics analysis of a multi -omics dataset on wild sea scallop populations from the Mid-Atlantic Bight to the northern Gulf of Maine. Specifically, the successful candidate will leverage our chromosome-level genome assembly to determine population structure and the regulatory

control of chromatin accessibility on transcription to a warming x acidification challenge. Results of this analysis will determine the molecular machinery underpinning adaptive responses to current and impending challenges relevant to the sea scallop fishery. The project spans population genomics, chromatin remodeling, transcriptomics and cytology and will benefit a range of stakeholders leading annual sea scallop

surveys and wild fisheries management in the US Atlantic (e.g. NOAA, Maine Department of Marine Resources, Commercial Fisheries Research Foundation, Virginia Institute of Marine Science). The PDS will be physically located within the Gurr Lab at HMSC in Newport OR, a vibrant new lab (gurr-lab.com) consisting of postdoctoral research scientists, two graduate students, and a varying number of undergraduates. Depending

on interest, there may be opportunities to become involved in other projects or to mentor graduate and undergraduate students. The PDS will be expected to present results at conferences and in meetings with stakeholders, provide written and oral reports to project partners, and to publish the scallop genome alongside results from our multi -omics dataset in peer-reviewed literature. This is a full time, 12-month

(1-year) position with possibility of reappointment for a second year, dependent on performance and funding. University of Washington Postdoctoral Scholar appointments are for a temporary, defined period not to exceed five years/60 months, including any previous postdoctoral experience.
