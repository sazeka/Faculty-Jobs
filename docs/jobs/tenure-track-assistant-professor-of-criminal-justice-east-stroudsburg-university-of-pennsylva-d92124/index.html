The Criminal Justice program offers a comprehensive foundation of core courses, in addition to a

wide variety of elective courses and opportunities for internships and experiential learning. Applicants must possess prior university-level teaching experience, and be able to teach both introductory survey courses, and upper-level major electives in an area of expertise. While all specialty areas will be considered, preference will be given to candidates who: Demonstrate a strong background teaching courses related

to the Courts/Judicial Process (to include areas such as bail, plea-bargaining, the trial & sentencing for example); OR topics of Social Inequalities, Crime and Justice (to include areas such as racial and ethnic disparities in crime and criminal justice, women in criminal justice, poverty and crime for example); AND an open secondary area of specialization in teaching and/or research.The successful candidate will

have a 4/4 teaching load and is also expected to maintain a focused scholarly agenda and provide service to the Department and University. The position also requires working with other department faculty in developing programming activities and events, as well as culturally responsive curriculum and pedagogy.Qualifications: A Ph.D. in Criminal Justice, Criminology or closely related program of study (ABD considered

provided dissertation defense date guarantees conferral of degree before date of appointment). Experience teaching Criminal Justice and/or closely related subject(s) at the post-secondary level.Evidence of ongoing scholarly activity.Minimum of two (2) years professional experience in Criminal Justice or closely related field.The ability to work with diverse populations.What We Offer Outstanding benefits package

including medical from date of hire, retirement contributions, employee wellness program, and paid holiday leave.Family Tuition waivers: Every full-time employee will receive a full tuition waiver for all ESU courses for themselves, spouse/domestic partner, and children.
