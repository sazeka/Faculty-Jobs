Translational Neuroscience - A Division-wide, Open-rank Faculty Search #BSD040

University of Chicago  ·  Biological Sciences Division  ·  Chicago, IL
FacultyNeuroscience

Position summary

Scope and Opportunity The University of Chicago's Biological Sciences Division is searching for full-time scientists , physician-scientists, and engineers whose work will advance understanding of the nervous system and accelerate the translation of discovery into new diagnostic, preventive, or therapeutic strategies for neurological, psychiatric, developmental, sensory, and neurodegenerative disorders . This search

builds on a highly successful, decade-long focus on building systems-level experimental, computational, and theoretical neuroscience at the University of Chicago. Academic rank, track, tenure status, and compensation are dependent upon qualifications. P rimary appointments will be in appropriate departments or units, most likely in the Biological Sciences, though potentially in the Physical Sciences Division, or the

Pritzker School of Molecular Engineering, with possible secondary appointments in other relevant units depending on the candidates' research. These positions are benefits eligible. The University of Chicago offers a wide range of benefits programs and resources for eligible employees, including health, retirement, and paid time off. Information about the benefit offerings can be found in the Benefits Guidebook .

Appointees will join a highly collaborative academic medical center with outstanding basic science and clinical departments, state-of-the-art core facilities, exceptional undergraduate, graduate and medical students, and opportunities to engage with new multidisciplinary research and commercialization initiatives, including Hyde Park Labs , a new hybrid facility for academic research, science-based companies and the

UChicago Science Incubator. Research Priorities The current search is intended to capture a broad range of neuroscience, including molecular, cellular, circuit, systems, computational, behavioral, clinical, and population-level approaches. We seek to recruit faculty who will strengthen connections among basic neuroscience, clinical neuroscience, human genetics, computational biology, engineering, and therapeutic

development, with the aim of creating transformative programs in human neuroscience and neurological disease. For the coming year, we are particularly interested in researchers whose work advances restorative neuroscience and circuit interventions, as well as researchers studying neuroimmunology, neuroinflammation and related brain-body mechanisms.
