The AuD program head is the most visible member of the program both within and outside the

university. As such, they are expected to articulate a clear vision for the AuD program and prepare students for the changing landscape of healthcare. The AuD program has strong ties to theworld-renowned Callier Center for Communication Disorders. The strong historic partnership with the Callier Center provides practicum placements, student scholarships, and support for shared education and research missions. Program

Head responsibilities include overseeing the AuD curriculum and training program, teaching graduate and undergraduate courses, and leading AuD program administration, accreditation, and review processes. In addition to clinical credentials, applicants must hold a Ph.D. in Speech, Language, and Hearing Science or a related field (Communication Sciences, Cognitive Science, Neuroscience, Psychology), provide evidence of

a dynamic and productive research program with extramural funding, and demonstrate a commitment to excellence in teaching. Applicant research programs may be in any area of hearing, balance, or associated disorders.
