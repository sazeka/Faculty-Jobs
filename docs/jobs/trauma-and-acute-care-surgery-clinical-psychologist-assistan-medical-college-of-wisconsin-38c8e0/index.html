Assistant ProfessorAssociate ProfessorPsychology

Position summary

The Medical College of Wisconsin Department of Surgery, Division of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery, is seeking a candidate for a Faculty Appointment as a Trauma and Acute Care Surgery Clinical Psychologist. This position is being recruited at both the Associate Professor and the Assistant Professor levels. The Division of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery (TACS) has an immediate opening for a Licensed Psychologist to join

its posttrauma mental health care program at Froedtert Hospital, an Adult Level I Trauma Center. This is a full academic medicine position that integrates three missions: clinical care for injured and acute care surgery patients, teaching through clinical and research mentorship, and building an independent program of research. Clinical care. The psychologists in TACS provide biopsychosocial assessment and treatment

services for patients with traumatic injuries who are admitted to the trauma service or to the acute care surgery service. Services are provided both on the inpatient services and in an outpatient trauma psychology clinic. Furthermore, the TACS psychology team is part of a multidisciplinary team that provides post-discharge care to firearm and other violently injured patients, that has also been recently extended to

a formal Trauma Recovery Center Model. The clinical care in this division leads the national in standard of care as well as innovative care models. Teaching and mentorship. The TACS psychology program trains clinical and research fellows, residents, graduate students, medical students, and other trainees. This faculty member will provide clinical supervision on the inpatient and outpatient services and research

mentorship to trainees, contributing to the division’s role in preparing the next generation of mental health, population health, and medical professionals to care for injured patients. Research. This faculty member will join a dynamic division engaged in training, research, and clinical innovation. The division is home to a strong trauma psychology research program, the Milwaukee Trauma Outcomes Project (MTOP), a

multi-institutional, NIH-funded research collaborative studying the mechanisms of posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and depression after injury and early interventions to prevent them.
