Job Summary APPLY BY EMAIL ONLY - APPLICATION INSTRUCTIONS BELOW We are seeking a highly motivated postdoctoral fellow to join the Wohl Lab at Brigham and Women’s Hospital at Harvard Medical School. The position will focus on developing and evaluating sampling strategies for pathogen genomic surveillance,

and on collaborating with international stakeholders to analyze pathogen genomic data. The Wohl lab uses genomic techniques to understand the evolution and spread of disease-causing human pathogens, conducting research at the intersection of infectious disease epidemiology, theoretical methods, and public health. We aim to harness the full potential of sequencing data to understand and combat human pathogens through

empirical study and methodological innovation. This involves working towards a more complete understanding of the evolutionary mechanisms of both viral and bacterial disease-causing pathogens, and also includes developing better ways to account for sampling biases in phylogenetic and other analytical results. We are also passionate about ensuring that sequencing capacity and study design expertise are in the hands of

local public health experts who are best positioned to take actionable steps towards responding to pathogen threats. The selected postdoctoral candidate will also work closely with the Grad Lab (https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/grad-lab/) at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. Principal Duties and Responsibilities: This candidate will work closely with collaborators in the Grad Lab to develop, simulate,

and evaluate genomic sampling and analysis strategies. Specific tasks may include: Developing a simulation framework for pathogen transmission and applying this framework to evaluate the epidemiological information gained under different genomic sampling scenarios Engaging with international stakeholders to identify priority public health pathogens and evaluate plausible genomic surveillance strategies Leveraging AI
