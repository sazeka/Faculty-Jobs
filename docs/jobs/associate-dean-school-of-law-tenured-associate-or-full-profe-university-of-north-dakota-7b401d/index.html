Associate Dean, School of Law, Tenured Associate or Full Professor 499677 Grand Forks, North Dakota, United States Full-time Faculty Salary/Position Classification $175,000 Annual, Dependent on Experience, Exempt 40 hours per week This position will work onsite at UND Grand Forks, ND campus Purpose of Position The University of North Dakota School of Law seeks an experienced administrator to serve as Associate Dean

for Academic Affairs and to join our faculty at the rank of Professor or Associate Professor (with tenure), as appropriate. The Associate Dean for Academic Affairs will play a key leadership role in the continued growth and success of the School of Law. Working closely with the Dean, the Associate Dean will provide leadership for the School’s academic programs and day-to-day academic and administrative operations,

and will serve as an integral member of the School’s leadership team. The position will be principally administrative (60%), with reduced expectations for teaching (20%, anticipated to be one course each semester) and research (20%). As the Associate Dean is the only mid-level administrative position in the School, with broad and significant leadership responsibilities, candidates must currently hold, or have

previously held, administrative roles in a U.S. law school—as associate deans, assistant deans, vice deans, and/or program directors, including in an acting or interim capacity. We will consider candidates who teach and research in any field. Candidates who can fill current curricular needs (which include, but are not limited to, criminal law, lawyering skills, oil & gas, property, torts, and wills & trusts), and/or

who can enhance offerings in our certificate programs (Aviation Law; Energy, Environmental, and Natural Resources Law; and Indian & Tribal Law) are particularly encouraged to apply. The School of Law seeks colleagues dedicated to fostering well-rounded and public-minded legal professionals with the necessary skills to serve as effective, self-reflective, and ethical leaders in their communities. Our distinctive

character as one of the Nation’s smaller public law schools—and the State’s sole law school—informs our thoughtful and collaborative approach to teaching and learning. We have a close relationship with the State’s bench and bar and a special interest in connecting with and serving our rural, urban, and reservation populations.
