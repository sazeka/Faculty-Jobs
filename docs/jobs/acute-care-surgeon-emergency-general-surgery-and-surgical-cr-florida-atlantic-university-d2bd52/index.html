Position Summary: The Department of Surgery at Florida Atlantic University (FAU) seeks a full-time academic surgeon who is board certified/eligible in General Surgery

and Surgical Critical Care. The primary responsibility of this position is to serve as a Surgical Critical Care physician, providing comprehensive management of critically ill surgical patients in the intensive care unit. The faculty member will also participate in General Surgery call and have a significant role in the education, supervision, and mentorship of FAU General Surgery residents. The faculty member will

report directly to the Chair of the Department of Surgery and work collaboratively with the General Surgery Residency Program Director and Department leadership. Responsibilities Serve primarily as a Surgical Critical Care/ICU attending, providing approximately 14–16 daytime ICU shifts per month, generally 12-hour shifts. Direct daily multidisciplinary ICU rounds and management of critically ill postoperative and

surgical patients. Manage complex critical care conditions including respiratory failure, mechanical ventilation, sepsis and septic shock, hemodynamic instability, multiorgan failure, acute kidney injury, nutritional and metabolic disorders, and complex postoperative complications. Perform appropriate critical care procedures within clinical privileges, including airway management, central venous and arterial

catheter placement, and other bedside procedures. Provide critical care consultation and coordinate care with surgical and multidisciplinary teams. Participate in ICU quality improvement, patient safety, and morbidity and mortality activities. Participate in approximately 4–5 General Surgery call assignments per month, including evaluation and management of acute surgical conditions, inpatient consultation, operative

care, and perioperative management. This is not a trauma surgery position; trauma surgery will not be a primary clinical responsibility. Provide direct supervision and bedside teaching of General Surgery residents in the ICU and during General Surgery call.
