Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is a field of expertise focused on the evolving relationship between people and computing systems. HCI researchers design and study new ways for people to interact, collaborate, create, work, and make decisions with technology, and investigate how emerging computing systems shape human

capabilities, practices, organizations, and society. Methods include the design and development of novel systems, experimentation, qualitative and ethnographic research, participatory design, computational methods, field studies, and mixed-methods research. We seek applicants who are defining the next generation of human-computer interaction: developing new ways for people to interact, collaborate, create, work, and

make decisions with increasingly intelligent computing systems. We are particularly interested in scholars who both imagine and build new forms of human-computer interaction and rigorously investigate how these technologies shape people, organizations, and society. We welcome research across HCI and related fields, including, but not limited to, human-AI interaction and collaboration, intelligent and adaptive

interfaces, social and collaborative computing, the future of work, accessibility, values such as sustainability, privacy, and justice in and through design, ubiquitous and embodied computing, information visualization, human-robot interaction, and emerging interaction modalities.
