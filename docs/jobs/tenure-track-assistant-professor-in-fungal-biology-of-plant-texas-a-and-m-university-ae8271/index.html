Back to Listings Tenure-Track: Assistant Professor in Fungal Biology of Plant-Associated Systems Plant Pathology and Microbiology Location College Station Open Date 8/11/2026 Position ID 188435 Description The Department of Plant Pathology and Microbiology in the College of Agriculture and Life Sciences on the main College Station campus of Texas A&M University invites applications for a full-time, nine-month,

tenure-track position at the rank of Assistant Professor with research (60%), teaching (30%), and service (10%) responsibilities. The expected start date is August 15, 2027. Applicants will be considered for the faculty title of Assistant Professor in the Department of Plant Pathology and Microbiology (https://plantpathology.tamu.edu/). The successful candidate will be offered a competitive salary, startup package,

and laboratory space in the Plant Pathology and Microbiology building. These facilities include state-of-the-art growth chambers and greenhouses, together with core facilities for Microscopy and Imaging, Genomics and Sequencing, High-Performance Research Computing, the Center for Structural Biology, the Biomolecular NMR Laboratory, and the Protein Chemistry Laboratory. Texas A&M University has historically had a

strength in fungal biology. This position is aimed at continuing this excellence. GENERAL DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES: The successful candidate is expected to develop and lead an independent, high-impact, extramurally funded, and internationally recognized research program in fungal biology of plant and plant-associated systems. Candidates studying fungi as pathogens, mutualists, endophytes, saprotrophs, or members

of the broader plant and soil mycobiome are encouraged to apply, provided the work connects to plant health or plant processes. Areas of interest include, but are not limited to, fungal pathogenesis and the molecular basis of fungal-plant interactions; plant immune and physiological responses to fungi and the mechanisms governing disease, resistance, or symbiosis; the structure, function, and assembly of plant,
