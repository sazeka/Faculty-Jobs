Position

description The Department of Radiology and Biomedical Imaging at the University of California, San Francisco invites candidates to apply for the position of Imaging Scientist Faculty in Molecular Imaging and Theranostics. The selected candidate will be appointed at the rank of Assistant, Associate, or Full Professor in the In Residence or Adjunct series depending on qualifications and interests. The new faculty

member is expected to develop an independent research program in molecular imaging. We seek an innovative scholar with expertise in preclinical molecular imaging, nuclear chemistry, radiolabeled biologics, peptides or small molecules, and theranostics. The candidate will work collaboratively with faculty within the Department of Radiology and Biomedical Imaging and other departments at UCSF to advance the development

of new molecular imaging agents, from early tracer development to first-in-man clinical studies. The successful candidate will establish and maintain a nationally recognized, externally funded research program, mentor students and postdoctoral researchers, and participate in departmental, university, and professional service activities. Required qualifications (to be met by the time of the faculty appointment start

date) include: Ph.D. in Radiochemistry, Nuclear Chemistry, Chemistry, Chemical Engineering, Pharmaceutical Sciences, Biology, Cancer Research or a closely related discipline. At least two years postdoctoral or equivalent research experience. Has demonstrated success in securing external research funding. Evidence of expertise in preclinical molecular imaging, theranostics, or nuclear chemistry. Strong written and

oral communication skills. Commitment to excellence in teaching, mentorship, and collaborative research. Preferred qualifications include: Experience with computational methods applied to molecular engineering or radiopharmaceutical development. Experience in the development and radiolabeling of radiopharmaceuticals for diagnostic and therapeutic applications using a variety of medically relevant radionuclides.
