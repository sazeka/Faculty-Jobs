Position description Professor Henri Drake in the Department of Earth System Science at the University of California, Irvine is seeking a postdoctoral scholar for an NSF-funded project that aims to use computational models to investigate the role of ice sheets in the global Earth System, with a focus on advancing understanding of two-way feedbacks between the cryosphere (grounded ice

sheets, floating ice shelves, ice bergs, and sea ice) and the ocean – and their role in global climate dynamics. The start date is very flexible, anytime from Jan 1, 2027 to Sep 1, 2027. The selected candidate will conduct cutting-edge research, including: Developing, running, and analyzing fully-coupled experiments using novel configurations of the Community Earth System Model (CESM3) with dynamic representations of

the Greenlandic and Antarctic ice sheets, utilizing two-way synchronous coupling to the Community Ice Sheet Model (CISM3). Evaluating and calibrate newly implemented parameterizations for basal melt and calving Developing and applying new process-based diagnostics to evaluate the fidelity of the simulations, grounded in available remote sensing and in-situ observations. Running mechanism denial experiments to isolate

the role of coupled feedbacks. The selected candidate will contribute to our research program and advance their own careers by: Contributing to a supportive, collaborative, and vibrant group culture Collaborating closely with CESM3 and CISM3 developers at NSF NCAR Mentoring both graduate students and undergraduate students on related projects. Disseminating their results through conference presentations and journal

publications. Position perks: This is a two-year position that is renewable, pending good progress and available funding.
