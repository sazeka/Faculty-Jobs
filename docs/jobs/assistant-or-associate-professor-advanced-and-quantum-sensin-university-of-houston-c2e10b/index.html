Assistant or Associate Professor - Advanced and Quantum Sensing for Geoscience and Energy Systems 497063 Full-Time Earth & Atmospheric Sciences Department: Earth & Atmospheric Sciences Salary: Commensurate with experience Description: The Department of Earth and Atmospheric Sciences (EAS) in the College of Natural Sciences and Mathematics (NSM) at the University of Houston invites applications for a Presidential

Frontier Faculty (PFF) position at the Assistant or Associate Professor rank in Advanced and Quantum Sensing for Geoscience and Energy Systems. The Presidential Frontier Faculty program is a university-wide interdisciplinary hiring campaign aimed at enhancing the UH research enterprise by integrating the strengths of existing faculty and infrastructure with strong early career new faculty hires. This

interdisciplinary position is intended to translate emerging sensing technologies into high-impact geoscience, environmental, infrastructure, and/or energy applications. Areas of interest include, but are not limited to, quantum-based or advanced sensing technologies applied to wellbores, surface, subsurface, airborne, spaceborne, laboratory, or infrastructure environments.Preferred candidates will have experience in

the application of AI and machine learning methods for processing and interpreting these sensing data to solve challenging geoscience problems in mineral exploration, hydrocarbon and geothermal energy, groundwater systems, environmental monitoring and geological carbon storage, natural and induced seismicity, fracture and fault-zone monitoring, infrastructure monitoring, planetary mission payloads, rock and fluid

characterization, and other subsurface energy systems. Candidates are not required to specialize in quantum-device fabrication but should demonstrate the ability to connect advanced or quantum-enabled sensing methods with significant geoscience and energy problems. The hire will join a growing campus-wide quantum effort, including two other concurrent quantum science-themed searches in the Departments of Physics and

Computer Science.The successful candidate will be expected to build active collaborations within and beyond the University, develop and sustain a nationally competitive, externally funded research program, publish research results in peer-reviewed literature, mentor graduate and undergraduate students, and teach graduate and undergraduate courses that connect foundational theory with practical applications.
